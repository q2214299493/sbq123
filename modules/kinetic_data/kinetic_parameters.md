Add `--format missing` for a gap report, `--format catkinas` for mean-field parameters, or `--format zacros` for directed lattice event coefficients. `--output FILE` writes once and accepts an identical replay.

## Sources and review

The network supplies species, phases, sites, stoichiometry, vacancies, direction, structure-bound thermochemistry and Registry provenance. Each entry in `models` selects a reaction and a Phase 1 parameter set. The binding file identifies the exact parent `network_version`. For parameter development, `parameter_input` can carry a new record following the existing Phase 1 schema. Published engine parameters require the selected set to have passed the existing network eligibility and artifact-review gates. A provisional parameter stays available for inspection.

The model carries the selected standard states, law for each direction, fixed coverage fractions when applicable, and sources with kind, reference and SHA-256. Supported sources include local and external calculations, literature, user values and explicitly approved assumptions. An approved assumption names its accepting user. A separate scientific review names a reviewer, timestamp with timezone and method. Its subject is `sha256_json([network_version, model_without_scientific_review])`. Evidence hashes come from supplied files or the upstream verified evidence manifest. This digest binds the contents; scientific authority and reviewer identity belong to the supplying workflow. The shipped example uses synthetic receipts and can be reproduced without a production database.

## Rate laws and numerical scope

* **Surface TST.** At the evaluated temperature, the coefficient at unit activities is $g\kappa k_BT/h\,\exp[-\Delta G^\ddagger(T)/(k_BT)]$. The positive-integer path degeneracy comes from the selected parameter record. Record explicitly whether multiplicity is already included in a measured prefactor or in the free energy. In that case use unit additional degeneracy. Supply a positive transmission coefficient. A coefficient above one requires an explicit tunneling model. Each temperature point needs its own compatible thermochemistry and condition binding.
* **Gas capture.** Molecular and dissociative adsorption consume one specified gas molecule and the declared vacant-site ensemble. The collision prefactor at unit gas activity is $s_0 A p^\circ /\sqrt{2\pi m k_BT}$. Supply the pre-activation sticking factor, effective capture area per reference site in m², mass in u, and their source. The documented Gibbs activation factor and degeneracy are applied once. An experimentally fitted sticking probability that already includes activation requires a consistent barrier convention. Dissociative adsorption can consume several vacant sites while remaining first order in the incoming gas.
* **Supplied prefactor.** The supported law is $g A_\mathrm{ref}(T/T_\mathrm{ref})^n\exp[-\Delta G^\ddagger/(k_BT)]$. The Phase 1 prefactor and thermal exponent determine the rate. A nonzero exponent needs an explicit reference temperature for this convention. Imported Arrhenius fits require their activation and prefactor definitions to agree with this Gibbs representation. Pressure-based coefficients are converted to the dimensionless-activity basis with the corresponding power of the gas standard pressure.
* **Coverage and uncertainty.** A term with expression `linear_transition_state_shift` adds its eV coefficient times the named fractional coverage to both barriers. This models a shared TS shift at fixed reaction free energy. The supplied coverage must lie inside the term's validity range. An `already_in_barrier` term is recorded once. An absolute one-sigma activation-free-energy uncertainty gives $\sigma_{\ln k}=\sigma_G/(k_BT)$ with prefactor, temperature and degeneracy fixed. A missing uncertainty remains null. A reverse rate obtained by equilibrium inversion has no assigned uncertainty without covariance evidence.
* **Direction.** Both independently specified directions must satisfy `ln(kf) - ln(kr) = -ΔG°/(kBT)` within an absolute log tolerance of 1e-6 on the dimensionless-activity basis. An explicit `from_equilibrium` reverse law uses a compatible forward coefficient and reaction Gibbs energy. Unknown direction permits a forward draft and leaves the reverse coefficient empty. Irreversible steps can have an eligible forward coefficient. Electronic barriers never enter the Gibbs rate calculation.

Gibbs values are measured in eV per elementary event. Calculations reuse the thermochemistry module's SI constants and eV conversion. Surface mass action uses fractional species and vacancy occupations. Gas activities use partial pressure divided by the identified standard pressure. Total pressure bounds the parameter's applicability. Reactor rates would also require an independently justified catalyst loading, site density and reactor normalization. [IUPAC transition-state theory](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/T06470)

## Export contracts

| Parameter table | Representation and conditions |
|---|---|
| CATKINAS | Each row has forward/reverse coefficients, gas partial-pressure orders in bar, site stoichiometry, Kelvin temperature, pressure in bar, physical Gibbs quantities and source binding. For reversible rows, `fixed_T_energy_input` gives the apparent Ea/G0 pair that reproduces the coefficients under the numerical one-bar Eyring convention at that temperature. Use the provided correction settings to avoid reapplying entropy, ZPE and collision corrections. A forward-only row has no native reversible energy pair. |
| Zacros | A local-event mapping supplies site IDs, a connected neighbor graph, initial and final molecule assignments, and the number of matching patterns per reference site for each direction. Each direction has its own per-occurrence coefficient. Gas pressure factors use bar and are applied by the consuming engine. The tabulated `pre_expon` is already evaluated at the stated T and coverage, so the point representation uses zero additional activation energy. A lattice adapter must preserve the site pattern, species ordering and occurrence normalization. |

The two JSON exports are parameter inputs to model assembly. A native CATKINAS model still needs its indexed equations and conditions. A native Zacros model still needs a matching lattice, energetics and simulation files. Temperature or coverage changes require regenerated point coefficients; a fixed-coverage table cannot represent arbitrary dynamic lateral interactions. Executing either model requires the existing downstream workflow.
